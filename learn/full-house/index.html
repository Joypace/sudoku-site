Full House: one empty cell.

Eight digits are already there. Find the one that is missing.

The idea

A house is one row, one column or one 3×3 box. Each completed house contains the digits 1 to 9 exactly once. When it has only one empty cell, count the eight digits already present and fill the missing one.

When that cell is also the last empty cell in the entire grid, the move is sometimes called Last Digit.

These are forced-move examples, so they start at Definite. The native highlight covers the complete house and its one missing digit. r2c1 means row 2, column 1; boxes are numbered left to right, top to bottom. Each play link opens exactly the starting clues shown in its animation; display settings may differ.

Example 1 of 3

Finish row 6: only 8 is missing

Row 6 contains eight filled cells and only one empty cell: r6c8.

The digits already present are 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 9. A completed row contains every digit from 1 to 9 exactly once, so the missing digit is 8.

Check the eight starting clues in this row
  • r6c1 = 9
  • r6c2 = 1
  • r6c3 = 2
  • r6c4 = 6
  • r6c5 = 5
  • r6c6 = 4
  • r6c7 = 3
  • r6c9 = 7
Fill r6c8 with 8

The whole row is now complete. Other rows, columns or boxes may still have work left.

Watch the full-unit highlight, then the double tap at the center of the empty cell. In Chill Mode the app fills the forced digit.
