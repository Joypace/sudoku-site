Naked Pair: two cells reserve two digits.

Two cells share the same two possibilities. You do not have to choose between them to rule those digits out elsewhere.

Look for two matching candidate sets

In one row, column or 3×3 box, find two empty cells whose only candidates are the same two digits. One cell must take one digit; the other takes the remaining digit. Their order can change, but together they use both.

Remove those two digits from every other cell in that same unit. The cells need not be next to each other. If either has a third candidate, this is not a Naked Pair.

Each example starts at Dilemma with no forced singles remaining. The native highlights guide your attention to the pair. Copied boards explore both assignments; Chill Mode then makes their forced placements visible. Double taps land at cell centers. Every play link opens exactly the starting clues shown here; your App’s display settings may differ. r2c1 means row 2, column 1; boxes are numbered left to right, top to bottom.

Example 1 of 3

1 and 9 are reserved in row 8

r8c3 and r8c8 are in the same row 8. Each cell has exactly two candidates: 1 and 9.

  1. If r8c3 is 1, r8c8 must be 9.
  2. If r8c3 is 9, r8c8 must be 1.

Those are all the choices for r8c3. Both arrangements use 1 and 9 inside the pair. Every digit appears once in a completed row, so neither digit can go in another cell of row 8.

These candidates are excluded in both tries
  • Remove 9 from r8c6.
  • Remove 1 from r8c9.
