BUG+1: the extra candidate breaks the two-choice trap.

One three-note cell in a board of pairs.

The idea

All empty cells have two candidates except one cell with three. If we ignore its extra candidate when counting, every missing digit must have exactly two homes in every row, column and box. If that reduced structure had a completion, replacing each originally empty cell’s value with its other candidate would preserve every given and unit constraint, creating a different completion. A puzzle known to have one solution therefore needs the extra candidate.

The uniqueness premise and the full-board two-home condition are both required. Every example here is independently verified unique and starts at Dilemma with zero singles and all ordinary candidates. No notes are edited to manufacture the pattern. The animations provide additional visible evidence: the other two guesses lead through ordinary forced moves to actual crowded-choice warnings. r2c1 means row 2, column 1; boxes run left to right, top to bottom. Each link opens the exact starting clues; display settings may differ.

Example 1 of 3

BUG+1: example 1

r2c9 is the only three-note cell: 2, 4, 8. Every other empty cell has exactly two ordinary candidates. The extra candidate is 2; the other two are 4/8.

For 2, the exceptional cell makes three homes in each of its units:

  • Row 2: r2c5, r2c7, r2c9.
  • Column 9: r2c9, r3c9, r8c9.
  • Box 3: r2c7, r2c9, r3c9.

The full-board check also covers every other missing digit in every unit. Two notes per cell alone are not enough.

Copy the original for each possible value. Only the first fill on each copy is a guess; every later fill is an actual ordinary single.

  1. Try 4 at r2c9. The ordinary forced fills are r2c8 = 8, r1c8 = 1, r1c7 = 3. Then r5c7 would have to be both 4 and 7. The app reports a crowded choice set. The animation stops at that first actual warning.
  2. Try 2 at r2c9. This extra-value trial remains an ordinary valid partial board; it is not presented as a completed solution.
  3. Try 8 at r2c9. The ordinary forced fills are r2c8 = 1, r1c8 = 3, r1c7 = 7. Then r5c9 would have to be both 4 and 7. The app reports a crowded choice set. The animation stops at that first actual warning.
The conclusion

Remove 4/8 from r2c9. Only 2 remains.

The visible warning here is a crowded-constraint contradiction, not a claim that the app detects the whole BUG pattern.
