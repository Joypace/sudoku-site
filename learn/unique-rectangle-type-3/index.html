Unique Rectangle Type 3: let the roof extras join a pair.

Let the roof extras join a pair.

The idea

In a puzzle known to have one solution, a two-box rectangle with X/Y floors needs an extra digit in at least one roof. If the roofs’ complete extra-candidate union is A/B, treat that required extra as one virtual position. An actual A/B cell that shares a house with both roofs supplies the other position. Together they reserve A and B in that house.

This lesson covers the virtual naked-pair form of Type 3. The broader technique can also use larger virtual subsets. Uniqueness is a required premise, and the virtual position is a reasoning aid—not an extra cell drawn by the app. Every example starts at Dilemma with zero forced singles. Its native highlight shows the complete named premises. Each play link opens exactly the starting clues shown here; display settings may differ. r2c1 means row 2, column 1; boxes run left to right, top to bottom.

Example 1 of 3

Unique Rectangle Type 3: example 1

The four originally empty corners, r3c1, r3c2, r7c1, r7c2, occupy rows 3/7, columns 1/2, and exactly boxes 1/7.

  • The floors, r7c1, r7c2, have only 3/4.
  • Roof r3c1 has exactly 3/4/9.
  • Roof r3c2 has exactly 1/3/4.
  • The real partner, r2c3, has only 1/9.

If both roofs used only 3/4, any completion would have a second completion obtained by swapping the rectangle’s two digits. The verified unique puzzle rules that out: a roof must use an extra, 1 or 9.

The roofs and real partner all share box 1.

A roof extra and the real partner must use different digits. So those two conceptual positions reserve 1 and 9. Neither digit can occur elsewhere in their shared house or houses.

Copy the original for every actual roof-extra possibility. These alternatives express the uniqueness-derived disjunction; they are not all the values of one starting cell.

  1. Try 9 at r3c1. The ordinary forced fills are r2c3 = 1. This remains a valid partial board.
  2. Try 1 at r3c2. The ordinary forced fills are r2c3 = 9. This remains a valid partial board.

Only the founding roof-extra placement is a hypothesis. All later fills are ordinary singles; the animation uses cell-center double-taps for them.

The shared conclusion
  • Remove 1 at r3c3.
  • Remove 9 at r3c3.

These are all the eliminations from every house shared by both roofs and the partner. Keep the pattern’s notes on the original; the overlays compare the actual trial boards.
