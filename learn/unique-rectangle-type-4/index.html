Unique Rectangle Type 4: a strong link rules out both roof guesses.

A strong link rules out both roof guesses.

The idea

In a puzzle known to have one solution, find four originally empty corners in exactly two rows, two columns and two boxes. The floors contain only X/Y, and the roofs also have extra candidates. If X can appear only in those two roofs within a shared house, neither roof can be Y: a Y guess forces a swappable four-cell rectangle.

Each copy tests and refutes its own roof guess. The actual four-cell uniqueness warning supplies the contradiction. These are separate refutations, rather than surviving alternatives that all exclude the same notes. Every example starts at Dilemma with zero forced singles. Its native highlight shows the complete named premises. Each play link opens exactly the starting clues shown here; display settings may differ. r2c1 means row 2, column 1; boxes run left to right, top to bottom.

Example 1 of 3

Unique Rectangle Type 4: example 1

The four originally empty corners, r5c4, r5c5, r7c4, r7c5, occupy rows 5/7, columns 4/5, and exactly boxes 5/8.

  • The floors, r5c4, r5c5, have only 3/6.
  • Roof r7c4 has exactly 3/4/6/8.
  • Roof r7c5 has exactly 3/4/6/9.

Across the complete row 7, digit 3 has exactly two places: r7c4, r7c5. This is the strong link; neither endpoint can be replaced by another candidate elsewhere in that house.

Test the two 6 guesses separately:

  1. Suppose r7c4 is 6. The ordinary forced fills are r5c4 = 3, r5c5 = 6, r7c5 = 3. The app then marks the four corners with its uniqueness warning. If this trial could finish, exchanging 3 and 6 in the four corners would preserve all givens and every row, column and box, creating a different completion. The verified unique puzzle rejects this founding guess.
  2. Suppose r7c5 is 6. The ordinary forced fills are r5c4 = 6, r5c5 = 3, r7c4 = 3. The app then marks the four corners with its uniqueness warning. If this trial could finish, exchanging 3 and 6 in the four corners would preserve all givens and every row, column and box, creating a different completion. The verified unique puzzle rejects this founding guess.

Only the first fill on each copy is a hypothesis. The next three corner fills are ordinary singles. The conditional swap does not exhibit two solutions of the original unique puzzle.

The two conclusions

Remove 6 from r7c4, r7c5. Keep their other candidates.

Each copy refutes the guess that began it. The animation holds both warning views before returning to the original. Its overlays show where the rejected guesses were made; the four-cell warnings establish the contradictions.
