Technique of the day · November 24, 2026

X-Wing: two choices, one conclusion.

You do not have to decide which placement is right. Explore both arrangements on copied boards and see the candidates they both rule out.

The idea to look for

Pick one digit. In each of two rows, it has only two possible cells, and those cells lie in the same two columns. The digit must occupy one diagonal or the other. Either arrangement rules it out everywhere else in those columns.

Swap rows and columns and the same argument works.

r2c1 means row 2, column 1. Count rows from the top and columns from the left. Each play link opens the exact starting clues shown here. Each example starts at Dilemma, with no forced singles left. Chill Mode highlights the moves that become forced after a trial placement. Your app’s display settings may differ.

Example 1 of 3

Rows 6 and 7 reserve columns 3 and 8 for 9

In rows 6 and 7, 9 has only these four candidate positions: r6c3, r6c8, r7c3, r7c8. They line up in columns 3 and 8.

  1. If r6c3 is 9, then r7c8 must also be 9.
  2. If r6c8 is 9, then r7c3 must also be 9.

These exhaust the two choices in the first highlighted row. Both arrangements occupy the same two columns, so every other 9 in those columns is excluded.

Remove candidate 9

r4c3, r4c8, r9c3, r9c8.
