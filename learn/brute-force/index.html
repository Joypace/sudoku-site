Brute Force: explore a choice, follow it, and backtrack

You do not need an advanced technique to ask a clear question and follow its consequences.

A guess starts a question

At Dilemma, the app has no single to fill for you. Choose one available candidate and ask what follows. Fill only what is forced. If that path contradicts a Sudoku rule, go back to the decision and rule out that choice there. If it reaches another Dilemma, choose again. Keep each deduction with the assumptions it depends on.

Copied boards: keep a small search tree in view

Tap an empty board slot to copy the current position. Make the first choice with the direction wheel, then double-tap forced cells or use Lightning to follow them all. A further copy can explore a child decision without changing its parent. Overlay the rejected try on its parent to inspect its consequences. Remove the rejected founding note on that parent. This example keeps the comparison boards: tap the child's overlaid thumbnail to unstack it before overlaying the parent on the original.

One failed child rules out that child choice, not its parent assumption. Only when every alternative at a child decision has failed can you reject the parent branch. Shallow search keeps comparisons readable; some puzzles require going deeper than the available board slots.

One board: history is your search stack

Try a candidate, then use Lightning to follow all forced moves until the next Dilemma, a solved board or a contradiction. Lightning follows consequences; it does not choose your next guess. At a new Dilemma, choose again and continue down one path. This is depth-first search, or DFS.

After a contradiction, double-tap Undo to backtrack to a Dilemma. The next-Redo cue identifies the operation you just backed past:

  • Dashed disc: the next Redo is a fill. It identifies the cell and candidate tried from this decision. Once its whole subtree has been refuted, remove that candidate here with the direction wheel.
  • Dashed ring: the next Redo is a note operation. This boundary does not identify a founding fill. Continue double-tap Undo toward the earlier decision, until the disc identifies the branch you have disproved.

Two-finger double-tap performs smart Undo on the play area; three-finger double-tap performs smart Redo. The Undo and Redo buttons also support double-tap. Redo lets you inspect an existing path again before changing it. A new edit replaces the redo suffix, so inspect the cue before removing the note.

The cue reports history, not whether a candidate is true. A warning-free partial board is not yet a solution. A fully valid completed grid finishes the search. To refute an assumption, every alternative beneath it must fail; one short trial is not enough. Every example starts at Dilemma with no forced singles and uses the exact starting clues opened by its play link. r3c3 means row 3, column 3.

Example 1 of 3

A shallow tree, visible on three boards

Keep the original safe. Explore a child on a second copy, then bring its refutation back one level at a time.

  1. Copy the original and try 4 at r3c3. Lightning reaches another Dilemma.
  2. Copy that position. At r1c3 the remaining choices are 3 and 6. Trying 3 produces a contradiction.
  3. Overlay the child on its parent. Remove 3 from that parent, then tap the child thumbnail to unstack it. The remaining choice, 6, leads through forced moves to another contradiction.
  4. Both child alternatives have failed, so the assumption r3c3 = 4 is impossible. Overlay its board on the original, remove that 4 there, and use Lightning to finish the puzzle.
Why the two paths fail

In the order shown in the animation:

  1. r4c2 and r4c7 would both need 3 in row 4.
  2. r2c6 would have to contain both 6 and 9.

Example 2 of 3

One board: a failed guess, a replay, and a solution

Use the fill disc to revisit a failed guess, inspect it with Redo, and solve on one board with Lightning.

  1. The starting candidates at r1c2 are 1, 6 and 7. Try 1, then let Lightning follow the forced moves to a warning.
  2. Two-finger double-tap Undo returns to Dilemma. The dashed disc identifies the tried 1. Three-finger double-tap Redo lets you inspect that same failed path; Undo returns to its decision again.
  3. Remove 1 with the direction wheel. Next try 7; Lightning also reaches a contradiction. Backtrack to its fill disc and remove 7.
  4. Only 6 remains at r1c2. Lightning follows that forced fill and all its consequences to a solved grid. No copied board is used.
Why the two paths fail

In the order shown in the animation:

  1. r5c3 would have to contain both 3 and 6.
  2. r5c3 would have to contain both 3 and 6.

Example 3 of 3

Follow the ring back to the founding fill

A note ring is not the founding fill. Continue smart Undo until the disc locates the assumption whose whole subtree failed.

  1. Try 6 at r2c3. Lightning follows its consequences to a Dilemma where r2c9 has 3 or 8.
  2. Try 8. After Lightning reaches a warning, smart Undo identifies its fill disc. Remove 8 in this conditional position; the remaining 3 also leads to a contradiction.
  3. Smart Undo now shows a ring for the note removal. Both child alternatives have failed. Continue double-tap Undo until the disc identifies the earlier r2c3 = 6 assumption.
  4. Inspect the saved path with Redo before changing it. Undo back to that disc, then remove 6 at the original decision. You are at another Dilemma, ready to continue the search.
Why the two paths fail

In the order shown in the animation:

  1. Row 4 has nowhere to place 1.
  2. r9c5 would have to contain both 1 and 9.
