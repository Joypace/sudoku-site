Pointing Pairs and Triples: a box reserves a line.

You may not know where a digit belongs inside a box. Sometimes you can still tell which row or column the box will use.

Look from the box toward the line

Choose a digit missing from a 3×3 box. If all its possible places inside that box lie in the same row, remove the digit from the rest of that row outside the box. The same argument works for a column.

Two possible places make a Pointing Pair; three make a Pointing Triple. Every possible place in the box must lie on that one line. Finding two aligned candidates is not enough if another candidate remains elsewhere in the box.

The cells do not need matching candidate lists. This is about the places for one digit in a box, not the two digits of a Naked Pair.

All three examples start at Dilemma with no forced singles remaining. The native box highlight shows every candidate for the chosen digit. Each copied board makes one hypothetical placement; no answer is inserted into the original. The play links open exactly the starting clues shown here; your App’s display settings may differ. r2c1 means row 2, column 1. Boxes are numbered left to right, top to bottom.

Example 1 of 3 · Pointing Pair

Box 5 reserves 6 for row 5

Box 5 needs a 6. Its only candidate places are r5c5, r5c6. They all lie in row 5.

  1. If r5c5 is 6, no other 6 can appear in row 5.
  2. If r5c6 is 6, no other 6 can appear in row 5.

These exhaust every place for 6 inside box 5. Whichever place is right, the box puts its 6 in row 5. So that same row cannot have a 6 outside the box.

Remove candidate 6 from r5c2, r5c7

These are all the 6 candidates in row 5 outside box 5. Keep the box’s original candidates: this argument does not choose between them.

What to remember: every place in one box lies on one line. The box reserves that line for its digit, so the rest of the line cannot use it.

Direction matters. Here the box confines the digit to a line. If the row or column instead confines its candidates to a box, that is the related Claiming pattern.
